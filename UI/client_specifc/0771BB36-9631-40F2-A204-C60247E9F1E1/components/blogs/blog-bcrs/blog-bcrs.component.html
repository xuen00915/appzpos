What is BCRS in Singapore? A Complete Guide for F&B Businesses (2026 Update)

  • 18th March 2026

Singapore is stepping into a greener future with the launch of the Beverage Container Return Scheme (BCRS) on 1 April 2026. Governed by the National Environment Agency (NEA) and operated by BCRS Ltd. — a not-for-profit consortium formed by Coca-Cola Singapore Beverages, F&N Foods, and Pokka — this nationwide initiative is set to transform the way Singaporeans think about their everyday drink containers.

At its core, BCRS is a simple "deposit and refund" system. When you purchase a pre-packaged beverage in a plastic bottle or metal can ranging from 150ml to 3 litres, a refundable 10-cent deposit is added to your bill at checkout. Return the empty container to a designated return point — such as a Reverse Vending Machine (RVM) or an over-the-counter collection point — and you get your 10 cents back. The goal is to recover over 16,000 tonnes of material from more than one billion drink containers used in Singapore each year, diverting waste from incineration and landfills. Learn more at bcrs.sg.

"Every container returned makes a difference for our future generations." — Senior Minister of State Janil Puthucheary. With approximately 1,000 return points launching island-wide from April 2026, 90% of HDB residents will be within a 5-minute walk of a return point — making recycling more convenient than ever before.

For retailers and F&B businesses, BCRS compliance means updating your invoicing and point-of-sale (POS) systems to handle the 10-cent deposit correctly. Critically, the BCRS deposit is not subject to GST — it must be itemised separately from the beverage price on all tax invoices and receipts. This means your POS system needs to be able to distinguish and display the deposit line item independently, so customers see full transparency at the point of purchase.
